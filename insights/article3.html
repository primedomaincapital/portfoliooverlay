The Overlay Manager Selection Guide for Institutional Allocators

Selecting an overlay manager? Compare capabilities in tax management, completion, reporting, and technology. A practical framework for institutional allocators.

Prime Domain Capital · Market Intelligence

The overlay management market is small, concentrated, and opaque — which makes selecting a provider harder than it should be. There are perhaps a dozen credible firms globally, the fee structures are bespoke, and the performance claims are difficult to verify independently. Yet the decision matters: the overlay manager touches every trade in the portfolio and sees every position. Choose poorly and you have added a layer of cost without removing any friction.

This guide lays out how sophisticated allocators evaluate overlay providers — the questions that separate genuine capability from marketing, and the structural issues that only emerge after the mandate is signed.

Start With the Operating Model, Not the Pitch Book

Every overlay provider will show you backtested tax alpha and elegant diagrams of their optimization engine. Treat these as table stakes. The real differentiators are operational, and they are harder to market — which is exactly why you should ask about them first.

Data ingestion. The overlay manager needs daily holdings and trade feeds from every underlying manager. How many managers can they onboard? What happens when a manager's custodian uses a non-standard format? How long does onboarding a new manager take — days or months? A provider that has integrated with 200 managers will onboard your eighth manager in a week. A provider that has integrated with 20 will take a quarter and bill you for the privilege.

Trade implementation. Who actually executes the overlay trades? Some providers run their own trading desks with direct market access; others outsource to a broker. Ask about their average implementation shortfall on overlay trades, their broker selection process, and whether they aggregate overlay trades across clients for better execution. The best providers will show you transaction-cost analysis. The rest will change the subject.

Tax lot accounting. For tax overlay mandates, the provider's tax lot engine is the product. Ask which accounting method they use as default (highest-in-first-out is standard for harvesting, but the details matter), how they handle corporate actions across the aggregated portfolio, and — critically — how they manage wash sales across managers. Then ask for their wash-sale violation rate. If they don't track it, walk away.

The Questions That Reveal Capability

"Every overlay provider claims 150 basis points of tax alpha. Ask them what their clients actually kept — net of fees, net of tracking error, over a full cycle including a bull market."

Beyond operations, several questions cut through the pitch:

"Show me a full-cycle attribution." Tax-loss harvesting looks heroic in 2022 and pedestrian in 2021. Any provider can cherry-pick a volatile year. Demand attribution across a full market cycle — ideally 2019 through 2024, which includes a crash, a melt-up, and a sideways grind. The honest providers will show you years where the tax alpha was 30 basis points. Those are the ones to hire.

"What is your tracking error budget, and who sets it?" Aggressive tax harvesting increases tracking error against the underlying managers' targets. The provider should have an explicit tracking-error budget — typically 50 to 150 basis points for tax overlay — agreed with you in advance, with escalation procedures when it is breached. If the provider can't articulate their tracking-error framework, they are harvesting blindly.

"How do you handle manager changes?" The overlay manager should make manager transitions cheaper and less disruptive, not more. Ask specifically: when we fire a manager, do you manage the transition in-house or refer us to a transition manager? The best overlay providers absorb transitions into the mandate. The weaker ones treat every manager change as a change order.

"Who is my team?" Overlay is a service business disguised as a technology business. You are hiring people, not software. Ask who will cover your account day-to-day, how many accounts they cover, and what happens when your portfolio manager leaves. Then ask to meet the backup.

Fee Structures and What They Signal

Overlay fees typically run 5 to 25 basis points on overlaid assets, scaling down with size. Tax overlay commands the higher end; completion-only mandates the lower. Be suspicious of fees below 5 basis points — at that level, the provider is either cross-subsidizing from another business line or not actually doing the work.

More important than the level is the structure. Asset-based fees align the provider's incentives with yours in a straightforward way: they get paid more when your portfolio grows. Performance-based fees on tax alpha sound appealing but create perverse incentives — the provider may harvest aggressively to maximize measured alpha while blowing through tracking-error budgets. Most sophisticated allocators prefer a flat asset-based fee with a tracking-error penalty clause: the fee steps down if realized tracking error exceeds the agreed budget.

One structural question matters more than fee level: is the overlay provider affiliated with any of your underlying managers? Several large asset managers now offer overlay services alongside their active products. The conflict is obvious — the overlay desk sees every manager's trades, including competitors'. Demand information barriers in writing, or hire an independent provider. Parametric's acquisition by Morgan Stanley in 2003 raised exactly these questions, and they have never fully gone away for affiliated providers.

The Decision Framework

For most institutions, the selection comes down to three archetypes. The tax specialist (Parametric/Morgan Stanley, Aperio/BlackRock) — deepest tax technology, primarily serves taxable wealth and family office markets, less institutional completion capability. The institutional generalist (Russell Investments, Northern Trust) — full tax and completion overlay, deepest pension and endowment client base, stronger on the completion side. The boutique — a handful of smaller firms with genuine capability and senior attention, but concentration risk if your mandate is large relative to their business.

None of these is universally right. A $5 billion public pension needs the institutional generalist. A $300 million taxable family office needs the tax specialist. A $150 million endowment with a creative CIO might do best with the boutique — and get the founder on the phone.

The through-line: hire the provider whose operating model matches your portfolio's actual frictions, verify their claims across a full cycle, and structure the fee so their incentives point the same direction as yours. Overlay done well is invisible — the portfolio simply costs less to run and keeps more of what it earns. That invisibility is the product. Make sure you are paying for the real thing.
