Overlay Management Fees & Tax Overlay Value — Market Data

An overlay manager's job is to make a portfolio behave — to hold its risk, currency, and tax profile steady while the underlying managers do whatever they were hired to do. It is quiet work, priced in basis points, and its value shows up in places that never appear on a performance chart: taxes not paid, trades not duplicated, collateral not stranded.

Public pricing for overlay mandates is thin, because most mandates are negotiated and never disclosed. What is public is better than nothing: a worked fee example from a major manager, and a stack of published research on the tax value that overlays and tax-aware implementation can add. This page reports both, and labels the worked example as exactly that — an example, not a market average.

Methodology

Included: the one fully worked public overlay fee example found (LGIM America) and published tax-alpha research ranges from Vanguard, Parametric, and J.P. Morgan. Excluded: any claimed 'average overlay fee' — none was found in a public source this session, and averaging negotiated mandates from a single example would be invention. Tax-alpha ranges vary with tax bracket, portfolio size, and execution; the range, not a point estimate, is the honest figure.

Trend labels: “Baseline (October 2026) — trend tracking starts with the next refresh” means no prior-period figure from the same source was available this session, so no movement is claimed. Where a prior figure is shown, the trend compares the current figure with that prior figure from the same source.
